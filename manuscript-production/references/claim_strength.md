# Claim Strength Reference

## Support Levels

Established: appropriate when supported by strong direct evidence, consensus, or guidelines.

Associated: appropriate for observational relationships or non-causal findings.

Plausible: appropriate for mechanistic or biologically reasonable interpretations.

Hypothesis-generating: appropriate for early, limited, indirect, or exploratory findings.

Overclaimed: use when the manuscript states or implies more certainty, causality, generalizability, or clinical benefit than the evidence supports.

## Common Softening Moves

- "prevents" -> "may reduce" or "may be associated with lower risk of"
- "causes" -> "is associated with" unless causal evidence exists
- "proves" -> "supports" or "is consistent with"
- "should be treated to prevent" -> "supports evaluation and treatment when clinically indicated"
- "reduces mortality/SUDEP/LOE" -> "effects on mortality/SUDEP/LOE remain uncertain" unless directly proven

## Reviewer-Facing Rule

Use the strongest wording the evidence can defend, not the strongest wording the argument would like.
