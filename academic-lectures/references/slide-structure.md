# Medical Lecture Slide Structure

## Standard Slide Order

1. **Title Slide** — Topic, presenter name, credentials, institution, date
2. **Disclosures** — Conflicts of interest (required for CME; use "None" if applicable)
3. **Agenda / Overview** — 3–5 bullet roadmap of the talk
4. **Learning Objectives** — 3–5 SMART objectives using Bloom's taxonomy verbs (describe, explain, apply, differentiate, manage)
5. **Take-Home Points** — *(best practice default: appears here AND at the end)* 3–5 concise key messages the audience should leave with; frame as "By the end of this talk you will know..."
6. **[Content Block]** — See content slide types below; order depends on topic
7. **Clinical Pearls** — Distilled, memorable clinical insights; use a visually distinct slide design (e.g., highlighted box)
8. **Summary / Take-Home Points (Repeat)** — Exact same points as slide 5; repetition reinforces retention
9. **References** — Numbered list, Vancouver/AMA format; cite only slides with referenced claims
10. **Questions / Thank You** — Contact info optional

## Content Slide Types

Use as many of these as needed within the content block, ordered by pedagogical flow (background → mechanism → clinical → evidence → management):

### Background / Epidemiology
- Prevalence, incidence, public health burden
- Risk factors, demographics
- **Visual:** data visualization (bar chart, map, trend line)

### Pathophysiology
- Mechanism of disease, molecular/cellular basis, cascade
- **Visual:** pathophysiology flowchart (step-by-step, labeled arrows)

### Anatomy Slide
- Relevant anatomy with clinical correlation
- **Visual:** anatomical diagram (labeled, clean, educational style)

### Classification / Staging
- Diagnostic criteria, grading systems, staging
- **Visual:** table (preferred) or decision tree flowchart

### Clinical Presentation
- Signs, symptoms, history pearls
- **Visual:** clinical scenario illustration (patient vignette panel) OR table

### Case Vignette
- Brief case (age, sex, presenting complaint, key findings)
- "What is your next step?" — pause for audience engagement
- Follow-up slide with answer and teaching point
- **Visual:** clinical scenario illustration

### Diagnostic Workup
- Lab, imaging, procedures — when and why
- Stepwise approach
- **Visual:** flowchart or table

### Evidence Summary
- Key landmark trials, guidelines (ACC/AHA, ACEP, Uptodate level of evidence)
- Study name, year, N, primary outcome, result, implication
- **Visual:** data visualization (forest plot, KM curve, results table)

### Study Results Slide
- Single study deep-dive: design, population, intervention, comparator, outcome, NNT/NNH
- **Visual:** data visualization

### Management / Treatment
- Algorithm, stepwise plan, dosing
- **Visual:** flowchart or table

### Controversies / Unanswered Questions
- Areas of active debate; helps advanced audiences
- **Visual:** None or simple comparison table

### Clinical Pearls
- 3–5 high-yield, memorable points
- Distinct formatting (bold, highlight box, or pearl icon)
- **Visual:** None (let the text speak)

## Audience Calibration

| Audience | Vocabulary | Depth | Case complexity | Evidence emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Medical students | Define all terms, avoid jargon | Foundational mechanisms + basics | Simple, classic presentations | Low — focus on concepts |
| Residents | Assume core knowledge | Clinical reasoning, management algorithms | Moderate complexity, common pitfalls | Moderate — key guidelines |
| Fellows | Subspecialty depth | Nuanced decision-making, edge cases | Complex, atypical presentations | High — trial-level detail |
| Attendings | Peer-level | Updates, controversies, guidelines changes | Rare/complex | Very high — primary literature |
| Mixed clinical | Plain language, define abbreviations | Practical "what to do" focus | Relatable, team-relevant scenarios | Low — actionable takeaways |

## Best Practices

- **1.5 min/slide** is the standard pacing rule
- **6x6 rule:** No more than 6 bullets per slide, 6 words per bullet (content slides)
- **Take-home points twice:** After learning objectives (set expectations) and at the end (reinforce retention)
- **Case vignettes:** Use at least one; they increase engagement and memory
- **Evidence slides:** Cite landmark trials by name (AFFIRM, RALES, SPRINT, etc.) — audiences remember names
- **Avoid reading slides aloud** — notes should be speaker-driven, not recapping slide text
- **Transitions:** Move from basic science → clinical application → management → evidence; avoid jumping between levels
- **Font:** Sans-serif (Calibri, Arial, Helvetica), min 24pt body, 36pt+ headers
- **Color contrast:** Ensure accessible contrast; avoid red/green combinations
